# Bibliography checks for revision 2

The reference list is contextual except where a cited tool is used explicitly. No exhaustive priority search is claimed.

- Arratia–Canfield–Hales: published citation is *Combinatorics, Probability and Computing* 32(4) (2023), 559–593, DOI 10.1017/S0963548323000020. Its macroscopic Poisson–Dirichlet theorem is background, not a proof input here. Primary preprint record: https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09183 .
- Stark: *Australasian Journal of Combinatorics* 86(3) (2023), 414–422. Theorem 1 on printed page 415 states the joint independent-binomial range through (n−1)/3. Source: https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/86/ajc_v86_p414.pdf . The current manuscript proves its stated larger elementary range rather than attributing that range to Stark.
- Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon: *Statistical Science* 5(4) (1990), 403–424, DOI 10.1214/ss/1177012015. Theorem 2 is the point-process approximation used in Section 11. Its total-variation norm convention is twice the supremum-over-events convention used here; with b3=0, a bound by 2(b1+b2) in the present convention suffices. Author copy: https://dornsife.usc.edu/larry-goldstein/wp-content/uploads/sites/221/2023/06/pacs-1.pdf .
- Arratia: the scale-invariant Poisson survey was published in 1998 in DIMACS Series volume 41, pages 21–41; arXiv:1611.05572 is an updated electronic version, not its original publication year. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05572 .
- Bhattacharjee–Molchanov: *Electronic Journal of Probability* 25 (2020), article 79, 1–20, DOI 10.1214/20-EJP482. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06229 . The Poisson-sum/Dickman connection is classical; the manuscript's contribution is the Thorp application in its proved range.
- The classical Erdős–Turán paper and Thorp's 1973 paper supply historical background. Long's Paper V supplies the related one-shuffle context within the destination repository. These are not substituted for any proof step of the main moment theorem.
